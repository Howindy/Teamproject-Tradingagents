## 1. Executive summary

Microsoft closed FY2025 (ended 2025-06-30) with **revenue of $281,724M**, **operating income of $128,528M**, **net income of $101,832M**, and **diluted EPS of $13.64**. The program metrics confirm **annual revenue growth of 14.9%** and a **quarterly (Mar-2025) revenue growth of 13.3%**, with very high profitability: **gross margin 68.7%**, **operating margin 45.7%**, **net margin 36.9%** in the March quarter.

Cash generation remains exceptional: **reported free cash flow of $71,611M** for FY2025 (program metric, exact source-currency amount), after **$64,551M of PP&E payments** against **$136,162M of operating cash flow**. The balance sheet is conservative — **current ratio 1.4x** and **liabilities-to-assets 44.5%** at 2025-06-30 — though both metrics deteriorated modestly versus 2025-03-31 (see §5, unverified derivation).

Valuation: **P/E of 38.9x** on FY2025 EPS of $13.64 (close of 2025-08-04). That is a premium multiple that requires continued double-digit growth and margin durability; the key swing factor is the scale of AI/datacenter capex, which is consuming a rising share of operating cash flow.

## 4. Cash flow and capital intensity

**FY2025:** Operating cash flow **$136,162M**; payments for PP&E **$64,551M**; **reported FCF $71,611M** (program metric, exact source amount — not a derived quarter).

**Nine months to 2025-03-31:** OCF $93,515M; PP&E $47,472M.
**Q3 FY2025 alone:** OCF $37,044M; PP&E $16,745M.

**Unverified derivations (assumptions stated):**
- *Capex intensity:* PP&E payments / revenue = 64,551 / 281,724 ≈ **22.9%** of FY2025 revenue — an unusually high ratio for a software business and the single most important fundamental variable for forward FCF.
- *FCF conversion:* reported FCF / net income = 71,611 / 101,832 ≈ **70%**; FCF / OCF ≈ **53%**. Roughly half of operating cash flow is being reinvested in property and equipment.
- *Implied Q4 capex:* 64,551 − 47,472 = **$17,079M** for Apr–Jun 2025, versus $16,745M in the March quarter — capex still climbing sequentially. (Derived by subtraction; not a program metric.)

**Takeaway:** The franchise throws off enormous cash, but the FCF growth algorithm now depends on whether AI infrastructure spend begins to monetize. Any guidance suggesting capex growth outpacing revenue growth is the primary downside risk to the FCF-based valuation.

## 8. Actionable conclusions

1. **Quality is intact:** 68.7% gross / 45.7% operating / 36.9% net margins in the March quarter, with annual figures essentially identical — no evidence of margin erosion from AI costs at the operating line.
2. **Growth is solid but decelerating at the margin:** 14.9% annual vs 13.3% quarterly revenue growth; the 0.6% QoQ print is the softest datapoint and argues for watching the already-filed June quarter closely.
3. **Capex is the swing factor:** ~22.9% of revenue going to PP&E (unverified derivation) and FCF conversion of ~70% of net income mean the FCF story is now a capex-discipline story.
4. **Balance sheet is a support, not a catalyst:** 1.4x current ratio and 44.5% liabilities-to-assets are comfortable, but both drifted slightly worse in the June quarter.
5. **Valuation leaves little room for error:** 38.9x FY2025 earnings prices in sustained double-digit growth; position sizing should reflect sensitivity to any guidance cut on cloud growth or capex-driven FCF disappointment.
